Abstract & Method Overview: We developed an automated pipeline to prioritize causal genetic variants from whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data. It combines sequencing-quality filters and allele-aware subtraction of common variants with local snpEff annotation, multi-source pathogenicity scores, ontology-aware (Resnik) phenotype similarity and compound-heterozygous pairing. Starting from 5,012,204 variants, the pipeline ranked first a pair of rare heterozygous variants in BUB1B, the MVA type 1 gene, consistent with a compound-heterozygous configuration (phase unconfirmed), with an EPCR of 0.9379 against 0.5627 for the next candidate.

1. Five-stage computational architecture

The steps run sequentially; each consumes the previous step's output, and the submission file is produced without manual editing:

STEP 1
Phenotype Extraction
8 HPO terms parsed from the clinical document; rhabdomyosarcoma is the strongest MVA anchor.
STEP 2
VCF Pre-Filtering
GQ ≥ 20, DP ≥ 10, PASS, AB 0.30–0.70 for hets, allele-aware dbSNP Common subtraction.
STEP 3
Annotation
Local snpEff GRCh38.99 (canonical transcripts), MyVariant.info and AlphaGenome; HIGH/MODERATE kept.
STEP 4
Phenotype Matching
Resnik similarity to the patient's HPO profile; BUB1B ranked #2 of all candidate genes (0.632).
STEP 5
Ranking & EPCR
gnomAD AF ≤ 1%, compound-het pairing and a weighted geometric-mean EPCR with tail decay.

2. Methodology details & filtering logic

  • Phenotype (Step 1): the 8 HPO terms come pre-tabulated in the clinical document. The strongest anchor is HP:0002859 (Rhabdomyosarcoma), an established cancer predisposition in MVA. HP:0200067 (Recurrent spontaneous abortion) is suggestive of chromosomal instability but has no direct HPO annotation to any MVA gene, so it carries little weight in the gene scores.
  • Quality control & common-variant subtraction (Step 2): calls were filtered on intrinsic metrics (FILTER PASS or missing, GQ ≥ 20, DP ≥ 10, 0.30 ≤ AB ≤ 0.70 for heterozygous calls), which removed 11.7% of calls. Allele-aware subtraction against dbSNP Common (b151, MAF ≥ 0.01) then left 591,339 of 5,012,204 variants (88.2% removed in total).
  • Annotation (Step 3): a snpEff GRCh38.99 database built locally from Ensembl release 99 (one canonical transcript per variant), MyVariant.info (hg38: gnomAD, CADD, REVEL, ClinVar, OMIM) and AlphaGenome Atlas (AVI_PHRED, SNVs only). 905 HIGH/MODERATE variants were kept. dbSNP Common (2018) misses most common coding variants: 712 of the 905 have gnomAD AF > 1%, so gnomAD is the effective frequency filter, applied in Step 5.
  • Phenotype matching (Step 4): each gene carrying a qualifying variant was scored against the patient's HPO profile with Resnik semantic similarity (HPO release 2026-09-01). BUB1B scored 0.632 (3 exact term matches), #2 of all candidate genes, and is the only Tier 1 MVA panel gene with rare HIGH/MODERATE variants.
  • EPCR (Step 5): four components in [0, 1] (phenotype 0.35, pathogenicity 0.30, inheritance 0.20, frequency 0.15) are combined by a weighted geometric mean, so weakness on any axis pulls the score down. MVA panel genes receive a 1.2× bonus; two rare heterozygous variants in one gene are paired into one row. The top score is capped at 0.95 and each following row is at most 0.6× the previous one, which separates a single confident answer from the tail, as F-max rewards.

Submission Nature & Automation

The submission file is the unedited, automated output of the pipeline: ranking, pairing and EPCR were computed programmatically. After generation we reviewed the top finding and the filters for plausibility (Section 4); this review did not change any score or rank.

Biological Rationale & Phenotype Correlation

BUB1B encodes BubR1, a pseudokinase of the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) that inhibits the APC/C to prevent premature chromosome segregation. Biallelic BUB1B variants that reduce BubR1 cause Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy syndrome 1: mosaic aneuploidies, growth deficiency, microcephaly and a high risk of childhood embryonal tumours, notably rhabdomyosarcoma and Wilms tumour. The child's rhabdomyosarcoma (HP:0002859) therefore matches the gene directly. Recurrent pregnancy loss (HP:0200067) is consistent with chromosomal instability but is not, by itself, specific to MVA.

4. Post-generation review & design choices

  • Quality audit: a spot-check of 300 common SNVs showed the dbSNP Common file misses many common coding variants; hence the downstream gnomAD AF ≤ 1% filter.
  • Expected effect of the pair: the stop-gain allele is expected to be lost to nonsense-mediated decay (an escaped product would lack the pseudokinase domain). The missense change lies inside that domain and is probably hypomorphic, with no functional assay yet. In trans, the pair would strongly reduce BubR1, the established mechanism of MVA1.
  • Scope: non-coding variants and incidental findings were not reported, to limit false discoveries; all ten rows are primary. Single heterozygous variants in genes with weak phenotype similarity (e.g. SGO1, MAD1L1) are penalized.

6. Pipeline evaluation, strengths & limitations

Key Strengths

• Scripted end to end; code and parameters in the public repository.
• Compound-heterozygous pairing within a gene, scored as one row.
• Score separation designed for F-max (one confident row, decaying tail).

Known Limitations

• Single-sample VCF: in trans phase needs parental testing.
• Depends on HPO gene–phenotype coverage (partial for some MVA genes).
• Non-coding regulatory variants are excluded.
• Two Step 3 runs differ (905 vs 1,526 HIGH/MODERATE variants); the cause is open.

Compute: with reference data downloaded and the snpEff database built, Step 2 ≈ 1.5 min (10 cores; ≈ 7 min on 2), Step 3 minutes (bound by API response times), Steps 4–5 < 1 min. 2+ cores, 8 GB RAM, 8 GB disk; no cost beyond hardware and free APIs.
